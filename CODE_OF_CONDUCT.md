# Code of Conduct

LudoWright aims to maintain a technical community where people can disagree openly without being hostile, dismissive, or abusive.

## Expected behavior

Contributors and maintainers are expected to:

- discuss ideas and implementations on their merits;
- give specific, actionable, and respectful feedback;
- distinguish facts, assumptions, preferences, and uncertainty;
- welcome people with different levels of experience;
- respect privacy, attribution, licenses, and project boundaries;
- avoid pressuring others to disclose personal information;
- accept correction and update claims when evidence changes;
- keep disagreements focused on the work;
- report safety and security concerns responsibly.

## Unacceptable behavior

The following are not acceptable:

- harassment, threats, intimidation, or stalking;
- discriminatory or demeaning language;
- sexualized attention or content directed at participants;
- personal attacks or sustained bad-faith argument;
- deliberate disruption, spam, or impersonation;
- publishing private information without permission;
- knowingly submitting stolen, unlicensed, or confidential materials;
- retaliation against someone who reports a concern;
- using project channels to coordinate harmful or illegal activity.

## Technical disagreement

Strong criticism of code, architecture, documentation, prompts, workflows, or product decisions is allowed and useful.

Criticism should explain:

- what is wrong;
- what evidence supports the concern;
- what risk or trade-off is involved;
- what alternative is proposed.

Maintainers may reject a proposal without rejecting the contributor.

## Scope

This policy applies to repository issues, pull requests, discussions, reviews, project-managed communication spaces, and public interactions where someone is representing LudoWright.

## Reporting

For immediate safety or privacy concerns, use a private channel such as GitHub private vulnerability reporting when appropriate. For other conduct concerns, contact the repository owner privately through an available GitHub contact channel.

Reports should include relevant links, dates, context, and screenshots when safe. Do not publicly repost harmful content merely to document it.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may:

1. provide a private or public correction;
2. remove content;
3. restrict participation temporarily;
4. block participation permanently;
5. refer security, legal, or platform-abuse matters to the appropriate service.

Enforcement decisions should consider severity, intent, impact, prior behavior, willingness to correct the issue, and participant safety.

Maintainers must not use this policy to silence good-faith technical criticism.
